AEGIS TECHNOLOGIES — INCIDENT RESPONSE PLAN
Effective: April 1, 2024 | Version: 4.2
Classification: Confidential — Security Team & Leadership
Owner: CISO | Review: Quarterly + Post-Incident | Test: Tabletop (Quarterly), Live (Semi-Annual)

1. PURPOSE
Define standardized process for detecting, responding to, and recovering from
information security incidents affecting Aegis Technologies' confidentiality,
integrity, and availability of data and systems.

2. INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION (SEV Levels)
SEV-1 (Critical): Active breach, customer data exfiltration, production downtime > 5 min,
                 ransomware, zero-day exploit in environment, regulatory notification required
  Response: Immediate (24/7), CISO + CTO + Legal + PR notified within 15 min
  Comms: War room (Slack #incident-sev1 + Zoom bridge), status page update < 30 min
  SLA: Containment < 1 hr, Eradication < 4 hr, Recovery < 8 hr

SEV-2 (High):   Suspected breach, lateral movement detected, credential compromise,
                 critical vulnerability exploited, customer-facing service degraded > 15 min
  Response: < 30 min (business hours), < 1 hr (off-hours), Security Lead + Eng Lead
  Comms: Slack #incident-sev2, stakeholder notification < 1 hr
  SLA: Containment < 4 hr, Eradication < 24 hr, Recovery < 48 hr

SEV-3 (Medium): Malware on endpoint, phishing credential harvest, misconfiguration exposure,
                 non-critical vulnerability exploited, failed attack with indicators
  Response: < 2 hr (business hours), next business day (off-hours)
  Comms: Slack #incident-sev3, ticket in Jira (SEC-INC-XXXX)
  SLA: Containment < 24 hr, Eradication < 72 hr, Recovery < 1 week

SEV-4 (Low):    Policy violation, suspicious activity (no compromise), scan/recon activity,
                 minor misconfiguration, failed phishing attempt (reported)
  Response: Next business day, assigned analyst
  Comms: Jira ticket only
  SLA: Resolution < 30 days

3. INCIDENT RESPONSE LIFECYCLE

PHASE 1: PREPARATION (Continuous)
- IR team: 6 FTE (Lead, 3 Analysts, Forensics, Threat Intel) + 24/7 on-call rotation
- Tools: SIEM (Splunk), EDR (CrowdStrike), NDR (Corelight), SOAR (Tines), Forensics (Velociraptor)
- Playbooks: 47 documented (ransomware, BEC, cloud compromise, supply chain, insider threat)
- Training: Quarterly tabletop, annual red team exercise, monthly purple team
- Threat intel: MISP instance, STIX/TAXII feeds, industry ISACs (FS-ISAC, MS-ISAC)

PHASE 2: DETECTION & ANALYSIS
Sources: SIEM alerts, EDR detections, user reports, threat intel feeds, vendor notifications,
         bug bounty, third-party breach notices, law enforcement
Triage (within 15 min of alert):
  1. Validate: True positive? (Enrichment: IOC reputation, MITRE ATT&CK mapping)
  2. Scope: Affected assets, accounts, data, network segments
  3. Classify: Assign SEV level, appoint Incident Commander (IC)
  4. Notify: Per SEV notification matrix (Appendix A)
  5. Preserve: Snapshot disks, capture memory, export logs (chain of custody)

PHASE 3: CONTAINMENT
Short-term (immediate):
  - Network: Quarantine VLAN, block C2 at firewall, revoke compromised tokens
  - Endpoint: Isolate host (EDR), disable user account, rotate compromised credentials
  - Cloud: Revoke IAM keys, modify security groups, detach compromised instances
  - Evidence: Preserve volatile memory, disk images, log exports before remediation
Long-term (sustained):
  - Apply patches, rebuild systems from golden images, rotate all related credentials
  - Implement compensating controls (WAF rules, detection signatures, allowlists)
  - Verify: No persistent access, no lateral movement paths remain

PHASE 4: ERADICATION
- Root cause identification: Mandatory for SEV-1/2, 5 Whys + timeline reconstruction
- Malware removal: Full disk wipe + reimage (no cleaning), verify with YARA/IOC sweep
- Vulnerability remediation: Patch, config change, architecture fix — validated in staging
- Credential rotation: All potentially exposed (service accounts, SSH keys, API tokens)
- Persistence check: Scheduled tasks, WMI, registry, cloud IAM, Kubernetes RBAC, GitHub apps

PHASE 5: RECOVERY
- Staged restoration: Critical systems → supporting systems → validation → traffic shift
- Verification: Synthetic transactions, data integrity checks, performance baselines
- Monitoring: Enhanced (100% log ingestion, 1-min SIEM queries) for 30 days post-recovery
- Customer communication: Coordinated with Legal/PR, template-based, regulatory review
- Service restoration confirmation: Business owner sign-off before SEV closure

PHASE 6: POST-INCIDENT ACTIVITY
- Blameless Postmortem (within 5 business days for SEV-1/2, 10 for SEV-3):
  Timeline, root cause, contributing factors, what went well, what didn't, action items
- Action Items: Tracked in Jira (SEC-AI-XXXX), owner, due date, CISO review monthly
- Metrics: MTTA, MTTC, MTTR, recurrence rate, detection coverage gap
- Reporting: Board (SEV-1), Executive Leadership (SEV-1/2), Customer (if affected)
- Regulatory: Legal determines notification obligations (GDPR 72 hr, CCPA, SEC 4-day, state laws)

4. COMMUNICATION PROTOCOLS
Internal:
- SEV-1: #incident-sev1 (all hands), Zoom bridge, executive summary hourly
- SEV-2: #incident-sev2, stakeholder update every 2 hr
- SEV-3/4: Jira ticket comments, weekly summary in #security-ops
External:
- Customer: Status page (status.aegistech.com), direct email for affected tenants
- Regulatory: Legal-owned, coordinated with outside counsel
- Law Enforcement: CISO + Legal only, FBI IC3 / CISA / local as applicable
- Media: PR-owned, holding statement < 1 hr, detailed update < 4 hr (SEV-1 only)

5. EVIDENCE HANDLING & LEGAL
- Chain of custody: Digital (hash-verified, timestamped) + Physical (sealed, logged)
- Legal hold: Activated by General Counsel, applies to all related systems/logs
- Privilege: IR communications marked "Attorney-Client Privileged / Work Product"
- Retention: Evidence 7 years, IR records 7 years, postmortems 3 years

6. THIRD-PARTY & SUPPLY CHAIN INCIDENTS
- Vendor breach notification: Contractual 24-hr requirement (DPAs, MSAs)
- Assessment: Vendor risk tier, data shared, access granted, blast radius
- Action: Access revocation, data retrieval/destruction verification, alternative sourcing
- Software supply chain: SBOM analysis, dependency scan, build pipeline verification

8. METRICS & KPIs (Reported Monthly to Leadership)
- Mean Time to Acknowledge (MTTA): Target < 15 min (SEV-1), < 30 min (SEV-2)
- Mean Time to Contain (MTTC): Target < 1 hr (SEV-1), < 4 hr (SEV-2)
- Mean Time to Recover (MTTR): Target < 8 hr (SEV-1), < 48 hr (SEV-2)
- Detection Coverage: % of MITRE ATT&CK techniques with detection rule
- False Positive Rate: Target < 20% of alerts requiring analyst review
- Recurrence Rate: Same root cause within 12 months = process failure

9. ESCALATION MATRIX (Appendix A - Summary)
SEV-1: IC → CISO → CTO → CEO → Board (if material) | Legal/PR parallel
SEV-2: IC → Security Director → CISO | Eng Director → CTO
SEV-3: Analyst → Security Lead → Security Director
SEV-4: Analyst → Security Lead (weekly review)

10. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT
- Postmortem action items: 90% closure within due date target
- Detection engineering: New rules from each SEV-1/2 within 2 weeks
- Threat hunting: Hypothesis-driven, informed by IR trends, monthly
- Red team: Annual full-scope, findings → detection rules → playbook updates
